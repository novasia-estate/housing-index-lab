What changes when you hold the mix fixed?

Observed median

Divide each quarter’s median sale price by the first quarter’s median. The series reflects the recorded basket of homes sold in that period. Switching toward larger or newer flats can move this measure even if prices within groups stay flat.

Fixed reference weights

Split records by town and flat type. Compare median prices within strata in adjacent quarters. Weight their log changes using the 2017 Q1 transaction counts and chain the results. A stratum needs 12 sales in each quarter. We suppress a link below 80% reference-weight coverage.

Hedonic model

Fit a separate log-price regression to each adjacent-quarter pair. Include floor area, remaining lease, storey band, town and flat type. Then chain the estimated time effect. We require at least 120 eligible rows per quarter and suppress estimates when the design is rank deficient.

Interpret the gap

Compare the last quarter’s index points across methods and read the sample and coverage table. A gap can reflect changes in the observed mix, model specification or measurement coverage. It is not a causal decomposition and the lab does not isolate the effect of individual amenities.

Market coverage

The source covers resale HDB flats in Singapore and uses registration dates. Incomplete quarters are excluded. The figures apply to the released snapshot and do not estimate prices in another country or for private Singapore condominiums. A continuous line ends when a required link fails.

Reproduce or extend the analysis

  1. Download the HDB CSV and record its retrieval date and SHA-256.
  2. Run python prepare.py source.csv --cutoff YYYY-MM-DD --out public/data.json with NumPy.
  3. Run the test suite and confirm the published totals, base period, coverage thresholds and missing links.
  4. Compare like periods. Search for the same flat type and date range before exporting a CSV to compare across notebooks.
